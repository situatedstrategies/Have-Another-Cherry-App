Have Another Cherry

Brand guidelines for anyone designing ads, landing pages, or marketing for the household expense app. Everything here comes from the shipped product — follow it and your work will look like ours.

Brand book · v1.0 · August 2026

The brand

At a glance

Have Another Cherry is a household expense splitter: couples and roommates set a percentage split, log shared expenses, and settle up. Money between people who live together is a touchy subject — the brand's job is to make it feel light.

Positioning: "A simpler, sweeter way to share household expenses."

Personality: warm, plainspoken, lightly sweet, quietly precise. Think a well-set kitchen table, not a banking app. The single glossy cherry against calm zinc grey is the whole idea: one drop of delight in an otherwise disciplined, minimal system.

Audience: couples, roommates, and families splitting rent, groceries, and bills — people who want fairness without spreadsheets or awkward conversations.

Logo

The cherry and the wordmark

The mark is a single photorealistic cherry with its stem — glossy, dimensional, appetizing. It is a photograph-style asset, not a flat icon: never trace, recolor, or flatten it.

On light grounds: use as-is
On busy, dark, or red grounds: white tile, 16px radius, 1px border
Have Another Cherry
Wordmark: Lora Bold, tracking −1%, title case, ink #18181B, with the tick beneath.

Do

  • Give the cherry clear space of at least half its own width on all sides.
  • Keep it at 24 px minimum on screen; drop it entirely below that rather than shrinking further.
  • Lock mark above or left of the wordmark, stem up.
  • Use the tick — the 44 × 3 px cherry-red rounded bar — under the wordmark and as a section underline. It is the brand's signature punctuation.

Don't

  • Don't recolor, outline, flatten, or add drop shadows or glows to the cherry.
  • Don't set the wordmark in any face but Lora, and never in all caps.
  • Don't place the cherry directly on the red flood — it disappears. Use the white tile.
  • Don't pair it with other fruit, illustrations of cherries, or cherry emoji in the same composition.

Color

One hue, its shades, and zinc neutrals

Cherry red is the only hue in the system — used at three strengths: Cherry, deep Cherry Ink, and the pale Cherry Wash. Every other color is a zinc-family neutral. Compositions should be roughly 90% neutral and 10% cherry — the red is punctuation, not wallpaper. Never introduce a second hue. One exception: third-party payment buttons (Venmo, Zelle) keep those services' own brand colors — theirs, not ours.

The red flood

For high-impact ads only, invert the ratio: a full Cherry field with white Lora type and a white tick. On the flood, buttons become white with cherry text, and the cherry mark sits in its white tile. Use at most one flood per campaign surface.

Typography

Three faces, three jobs

All three are free on Google Fonts. No substitutions in digital work; if a print vendor can't load them, fall back to Georgia / Helvetica — never to another decorative face.

Shape & surface

Soft corners, hairline borders, almost no shadow

Surfaces read as calm and precise: white cards with 1px borders and 16px corners on a Paper ground. Depth comes from borders, not shadows — shadows stay whisper-quiet (6–8% ink, small blur).

Voice

Warm, plain, one pun maximum

We talk about money the way good housemates do: directly, kindly, and without jargon or guilt. The cherry gives us "sweet" — use it lightly, at most once per piece, and never force a second fruit pun.

On brand
"A simpler, sweeter way to share household expenses."
Off brand
"Revolutionize your financial cohabitation workflow."
On brand
"Make sharing expenses sweet (or sweeter)."
Off brand
"Stop fighting about money!! 🍒🍒🔥"
On brand
"You paid $62.10. Sam owes $24.84."
Off brand
"Your outstanding receivable balance has been computed."
On brand
"We don't sell or unnecessarily store your data."
Off brand
"Bank-grade military-level security."

Rules of thumb: sentence case everywhere except micro-labels. Buttons say exactly what happens ("Join the group", "Add expense"). Show real, specific numbers in ads — precision is part of the charm. No exclamation points about money; the privacy line above may be quoted verbatim.

Ads

Rules for campaign work

Do

  • Ground every layout in Paper #F4F4F5 or White — or, for one hero unit per campaign, the red flood.
  • Lead with a real moment: a split ("60/40"), a settled balance, an invite code in its dashed box.
  • Use one Cherry CTA per unit, Inter 600 white on red, 10px radius or pill.
  • Let whitespace carry the luxury — the brand is minimal and high-contrast, so leave room.
  • End on the tick or the cherry mark as the sign-off.

Don't

  • No gradients, no second hue, no pastel pinks or "cherry blossom" drift.
  • No stock photos of couples arguing over bills, piggy banks, or cash fans.
  • No cherry emoji 🍒 in designed surfaces (social copy text is the one exception).
  • No drop shadows heavier than 8% ink, no glass-morphism, no dark mode ads — the brand is a light-mode brand.
  • Never render fake UI with numbers in a proportional font — money is always mono.
